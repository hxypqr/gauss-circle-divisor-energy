ORDERED CURVATURE, JOINT SPACING, AND HIGHER
                    JETS
        IN THE GAUSS CIRCLE PROBLEM

                                       XIYU HU


        Abstract. Li and Yang improved the pointwise exponent in the Gauss
        circle and Dirichlet divisor problems by combining a new first-spacing
        estimate, obtained from small-cap decoupling for the cone, with Huxley’s
        second-spacing theory inside the Bombieri–Iwaniec method. This note
        audits the main transformations in that argument and isolates the steps
        at which a further power saving can plausibly remain. The audit suggests
        that the first-spacing problem is already close to its natural square-root
        floor and cannot by itself cross the apparent 5/16 barrier within the
        present parameter architecture. The larger unexploited structure occurs
        earlier and at the large-sieve interface: the short sums are ordered pieces
        of one curved trajectory, while the double large sieve replaces their joint
        oscillatory geometry by unordered pair energies and separate worst-case
        estimates on the two sides.
           We formulate three research directions. The first is an ordered
        short-block theory, based on fixed-shift correlations and a finite-scale
        transversality-versus-rigidity dichotomy. The second is a curvature-
        weighted joint moment that keeps first and second spacing coupled and
        retains the fourth Poisson coordinate. The third uses higher Taylor
        jets of the reciprocal phase together with the translation-orbit inverse
        theorem for large Weyl sums. For F (t) = t−1 , the normalized coefficient
        jets form an anisotropically scaled moment curve and satisfy exact recur-
        rences among consecutive coefficients. This gives a concrete reduction
        of an exceptional large block to a thin arithmetic resonance relation.
        The statements labeled as targets, conjectures, or model problems are
        proposed directions rather than established theorems. This expanded
        version supplies proofs of the elementary geometric and arithmetic re-
        ductions, separates quoted unpublished inputs from their consequences,
        and records the exact analytic verification problem for an amplitude-
        dependent first-spacing proposal.

Background and the scope of this audit. The method developed by
Bombieri and Iwaniec [1] replaces a long oscillatory sum by short sums whose
local slopes admit rational approximations. Poisson summation then supplies
a dual family of frequencies, and a double large sieve measures how much
orthogonality can be recovered from spacing on the original and dual sides.
Later second-spacing work of Huxley [6] uses all four proximity conditions
and resonance curves. The decoupling viewpoint makes the dual first-spacing
geometry accessible to Fourier-analytic estimates; Bourgain–Watt’s work
on perturbed cones [2] is part of this development. Li–Yang’s particular
improvement uses Guth–Maldague small-cap decoupling, a consequence of
an amplitude-dependent wave-envelope theorem [4].
   These references play different roles. The historical architecture explains
which objects occur. Theorems quoted below are used only with their
stated parameter and regularity hypotheses. By contrast, the proposed
ordered, joint-spacing, and higher-jet mechanisms are research programs, not
consequences of the existing decoupling theorems alone.


What this version adds. The elementary expansions and determinant
calculations in the main text and appendices are proved here. The Weyl
inverse theorem is quoted from the supplied unpublished manuscript [5];
deductions using it are conditional on that input, not an independent cer-
tification of that manuscript. A separate anonymous candidate argument
proposes a numerical first-spacing improvement. Its mechanism is recorded in
Section 8.1, but its unresolved analytic compatibility check is not promoted
to a theorem in this methods audit.
   Several qualifications matter. A square-root lower obstruction is a state-
ment about the worst case over coefficients, not about every bounded coef-
ficient array. The smooth jet trajectory should not be identified with the
discontinuous modular coordinates of the Poisson points. Consecutive block
approximants need not be neighboring Farey fractions. Unit Taylor-phase
accuracy does not by itself preserve a small relative large-value threshold.
The precise versions are given below and collected in Appendix H.
   The purpose of this note is not to reprove the Li–Yang theorem. It is to
identify which parts of the proof are already quantitatively close to sharp,
and which parts discard structure large enough to plausibly support a genuine
new exponent. The main conclusions of the audit are as follows.
  (i) The reduction from the lattice-point problem to a dyadic double expo-
      nential sum is essentially lossless up to logarithms and a natural Fourier
      cutoff.
 (ii) The choice of short-block length N and the later Huxley second-spacing
      estimate may admit quantitative refinements, but no additional large
      conceptual loss is visible there.
(iii) The Li–Yang first-spacing estimate is close to its unavoidable square-
      root floor. Even a formally perfect first-spacing estimate, while all other
                   ORDERED CURVATURE AND HIGHER JETS                         5

     inputs are held fixed, gives only a small numerical improvement and
     does not cross 5/16 in the range 4 ≤ q ≤ 4.5.
(iv) The first major structural loss occurs when the ordered sum of short
     blocks is replaced by positive blockwise information. This weakens
     cancellation caused by the systematic rotation of the tangent and
     curvature of the original long phase.
 (v) The second major structural loss occurs at the double-large-sieve in-
     terface. A joint Fourier matrix is bounded by a product of first- and
     second-spacing quantities. The order of the short blocks, the correla-
     tion between the two sides, and part of the four-dimensional Poisson
     geometry are no longer visible.
   A second input is the inverse theorem developed in [5]. Its mechanism is
based on all cuts of one large Weyl sum, a critical maximal Vinogradov mean
value estimate on a fixed-leading-coefficient fibre, anisotropic sampling, and
a collision in a polynomial translation orbit. This mechanism is particularly
relevant here because the short blocks in the circle problem are themselves
translations of one phase. The main proposal is therefore not to replace
cone decoupling by a one-dimensional Weyl estimate. Rather, one should
move the translation-orbit and large-value structure to an earlier point in
the Bombieri–Iwaniec information flow.
   The three principal directions are:
 D1. an ordered large-value or T T ∗ theory for the family of short blocks;
 D2. a joint first–second spacing moment, before Hölder separates the two
     geometries;
 D3. a higher-jet decomposition of the reciprocal phase, followed by the
     simultaneous rational approximation theorem from [5].
The first two directions address the loss explicitly emphasized by Li and Yang:
the large sieve retrieves only part of the orthogonality between the short
sums [7, Introduction]. The third direction supplies a concrete arithmetic
normal form for exceptional blocks and makes direct use of the new Weyl
inverse theorem.

                    3. Audit of the available room
3.1. Steps with no visible large power loss. The following operations
are either exact, naturally scaled, or cost only T ε :
 (a) the hyperbola or lattice-point reduction to sawtooth sums;
 (b) the truncated Fourier expansion with cutoff (2.5);
 (c) dyadic decomposition in h and m and partial summation of the 1/h
      weight;
 (d) dyadic pigeonholing of the rational denominator and numerator;
 (e) the local Taylor expansion and Poisson transform, provided the block
      length satisfies the stated remainder conditions;
  (f) boundedly many decompositions of complex weights into positive pieces.
These operations should not be the first targets of a new approach.

4.2. Why the mechanism, rather than only the bound, matters. The
proof in [5] uses all cuts of a full-length sum. For each cut, either the prefix
or the tail remains large. After reversing prefixes, this supplies linearly many
large partial sums with a fixed leading coefficient, while the lower coefficient
vectors lie on one integer translation orbit. A critical maximal Vinogradov
mean value estimate on this fixed-leading-coefficient fibre and an anisotropic
sampling theorem force two orbit points to collide at the canonical scales
N −j . The triangular translation action converts the difference of the two
translation parameters into a preliminary common denominator, which is
then compressed to the scale in (4.4).
   This is precisely the type of information that disappears if the short blocks
in (3.12) are immediately treated as an unordered family. The transferable
principle is the following.
Principle 4.3. When many local polynomial sums arise by translating one
global phase, first seek a large-value estimate for the entire translation family.
Use the geometry of the coefficient orbit before applying a pointwise inverse

   The Li–Yang proof is not visibly losing a large power at every technical
step. Its first-spacing theorem is close to the natural square-root limit, the
short-block length is optimized within the positive second-spacing framework,
and the later resonance-curve estimate is a quantitative rather than a newly
created oscillatory loss. The substantial remaining room is concentrated
24                                    XIYU HU

where coherent information is converted into positive energy:

                      X                 X
                            Sj    ⇝          |Sj | ,
                       j                 j                                (10.1)
      joint (X, Y ) geometry      ⇝     first spacing × second spacing.
   The reciprocal phase provides more structure than a generic smooth phase.
Its Taylor coefficients lie on an anisotropically scaled moment curve, its
consecutive coefficients satisfy exact recurrences, and its rational resonances
are swept through in an ordered fashion. The translation-orbit inverse
theorem from [5] supplies a mechanism for converting large local values into
simultaneous rational structure. These facts point to a common strategy:

     Localize enough to expose arithmetic structure, but retain the
     block parameter, the curved jet trajectory, and the joint Poisson    (10.2)

       Appendix D. Farey adjacency: an exact identity and its
                                  limitation
  Suppose three fractions are genuine neighboring fractions in one Farey
sequence, in increasing order. They satisfy
                 aj rj−1 − aj−1 rj = 1,               aj+1 rj − aj rj+1 = 1.
Reduction modulo rj gives
                              aj ≡ rj−1 ≡ −rj+1            (mod rj ).                           (D.1)
                   ORDERED CURVATURE AND HIGHER JETS                             29

Also the two vectors (aj−1 , rj−1 ) and (aj , rj ) form an integer basis of Z2 .
Express (aj+1 , rj+1 ) in that basis and use the determinant relations. The
coefficient of the first vector is −1, so for an integer bj ,
                   (aj+1 , rj+1 ) = bj (aj , rj ) − (aj−1 , rj−1 ).           (D.2)
In particular, rj+1 = bj rj − rj−1 .
   These identities are exact, but they cannot be imposed on an arbitrary
ordered subsequence of rational approximants. For example, 1/5 and 1/3
occur in increasing order in the Farey sequence of order five, but after the
intermediate 1/4 is skipped their determinant is 5 − 3 = 2, not one. The
congruence 1 ≡ −3 (mod 5) would be false.
   The rational approximants selected from successive Bombieri–Iwaniec
blocks may skip intermediate Farey fractions, and dyadic restrictions can
delete still more. An ordered arithmetic argument must therefore retain
insertion/deletion data or products of the intermediate unimodular transition
matrices. The actual problem is to quantify how complicated this induced
transition can be while the reciprocal jets are simultaneously resonant. For-
mula (D.1) is a useful local model, not a proven recurrence for the selected
X-set.

           Appendix H. Revision and dependency ledger
   This expanded version retains the original note’s architecture: audit the
transformations, distinguish quantitative refinements from structural losses,
and formulate ordered, joint-spacing, and higher-jet targets. The following
qualifications explain changes to shorthand statements in the working version.
Worst-case lower bounds. The square-root floor refers to the best uniform
upper bound over unit-modulus or unrestricted bounded coefficient arrays.
It is not a lower bound for every array. The exact L2 calculation is given in
Section 3.
Smooth and arithmetic coordinates. The reciprocal Taylor coefficients lie on
a smooth analytic curve. The modular inverses and integer-part data in the
Poisson coordinates may jump. Equations (3.15) and (6.7) retain both parts
rather than identifying the full arithmetic X-set with a smooth curve.
Four-dimensional geometry. Appendix C proves the moment-cone identity,
the positive two-atom uniqueness statement, the Jacobian formula, and the
continuous bilinear estimate. The discrete sampling gain is deliberately not
asserted. Radial degeneracies and the scale at which the missing coordinate
is visible remain important.
Farey identities. Appendix D proves the neighbor congruence only under
its determinant-one hypothesis and gives an explicit skipped-neighbor coun-
terexample. No such adjacency has been established for the selected block
approximants.
                     ORDERED CURVATURE AND HIGHER JETS                                  37

Taylor transfer and integrality. Appendix E distinguishes direct phase sub-
traction from extraction of a large polynomial prefix. It proves that bounded-
variation transfer at the unit-error scale is sufficient for the quoted inverse
input, with no extra loss in the coefficient scales. Exact numerator recur-
rences require an integer center or a separately justified clearing of fixed
denominators.
Status of the inverse theorem. The sharpened inverse statement and short-
interval Weyl bound are quoted from the supplied unpublished manuscript
[5]. The elementary reductions in Appendix F explain its mechanism. The
compression input and the theorem as a whole are not represented as inde-
pendently verified in this note.
Status of the first-spacing candidate. The amplitude-dependent proposal
is confined to Section 8.1 and Appendix G. Its numerical consequence is
conditional on a concrete localization/compatibility assertion. It is not a
proved record, and it does not exploit the ordered-block or joint-spacing
ideas that motivate the main research program.
External framework and withdrawn precursors. The relevant replacement
interface is the one stated in [7, Lemma 4.4]. A distinct Bourgain–Watt
preprint [8] was withdrawn after errors were identified in first-spacing asser-
tions. Its main conclusions are not used as valid inputs here. This is separate
from the published perturbed-cone paper [2].
What a future improvement must demonstrate. Retaining more information is
not by itself an estimate. An ordered-block argument must prove cancellation
or sparse persistent resonance. A joint-spacing argument must exclude
simultaneous saturation on the two sides. A higher-jet argument must
count the resulting arithmetic resonances uniformly in h and restore the
full block sum. A first-spacing replacement must survive all denominator
scales, both block-length cases, and the endpoint ranges. These are the
explicit mathematical tasks, rather than assertions that the existing method
necessarily loses a recoverable power.
